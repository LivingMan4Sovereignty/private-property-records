PEACEFUL PEOPLE • STRONG PRINCIPLES • BRIGHTER TOMORROWS

1. My Personal Declaration

I am Colby J. Clauss, a living man created by God, possessing inherent rights that I believe exist independently of government permission.

I recognize my responsibility to live with integrity, honesty, accountability, and respect for others.

I believe government exists to protect the rights of the people, not to convert those rights into privileges granted or withdrawn at the discretion of public officials.

My guiding declaration is:

“I do not trade my God Given Rights for government one sided contractual privileges.”

This statement expresses my deeply held principles and my expectation that interactions involving me as the living man that I am, my property, and liberty be conducted lawfully, fairly, and respectfully.

2. My Foundational Beliefs

I stand upon principles of natural rights, common law, constitutional government, individual liberty, private property, and due process.

I believe legitimate government authority must remain accountable to the people and subject to constitutional limitations.

I do not believe a person's worth, dignity, or inherent humanity depends upon government-issued identification, occupational status, or participation in a particular administrative system.

I value voluntary agreements entered into with informed understanding and meaningful consent.

I oppose arbitrary exercises of authority, unequal treatment, coercion, and the misuse of governmental power.

I believe the constitutional structure of our republic should protect individual freedom while maintaining equal accountability under the law.

3. My Commitment to Peaceful Conduct

I desire to live peacefully among my neighbors and within my community.

I respect the lives, property, dignity, and legitimate rights of others.

I accept personal responsibility for my conduct and recognize that liberty carries corresponding responsibilities.

My commitment to protecting my rights does not represent hostility toward another person or a desire for confrontation.

I favor respectful communication, transparency, peaceful resolution of disagreements, and lawful processes for addressing disputes.

I seek neither conflict nor special treatment. I seek fairness, accountability, and the peaceful enjoyment of my life and property.

4. Constitutional Protections and Legal Principles

I value the protections established by the United States Constitution, including:

  • First Amendment: Freedom of religion, expression, peaceful assembly, and petition.
  • Fourth Amendment: Protection against unreasonable searches and seizures.
  • Fifth Amendment: Due process protections and safeguards concerning compelled self-incrimination.
  • Ninth Amendment: Recognition that enumerating certain rights does not deny or disparage others retained by the people.
  • Tenth Amendment: Recognition of the constitutional allocation of powers between federal and state governments.

I believe these protections deserve careful consideration whenever government authority is exercised.

5. Notice to Public Officials and Others

To any officer, government representative, or other individual reviewing this declaration:

I ask that our interactions be conducted professionally, peacefully, and with mutual respect.

When an official action affects my liberty or property, I respectfully request identification of the applicable legal authority and the factual basis for that action.

I ask that my questions and concerns be treated seriously and that applicable constitutional protections and procedural safeguards be observed.

I intend to communicate respectfully and pursue disagreements through peaceful and lawful means.

This document records my personal identity, beliefs, intentions, and requests. It is not presented as a government-issued credential or as a substitute for a judicial determination concerning any disputed legal obligation.

6. My Statement of Commitment

I choose to live according to principles of integrity, responsibility, independence, compassion, and peaceful coexistence.

I believe a free society depends upon people who respect one another's rights and hold themselves accountable for their actions.

I will continue to seek understanding, examine the law, ask questions, and advocate for the preservation of individual liberty.

My desire is simple:

To live freely and peacefully, to respect others, to protect my family and property, and to be treated with the same dignity and fairness that I extend to those around me.

7. Founding Documents and Judicial References

The following references are provided for further reading. The personal declarations above express my convictions; the summaries below describe specific historical documents and judicial holdings.

Declaration of Independence (1776)

Describes unalienable rights and the principle that governments derive just powers from the consent of the governed. It is a foundational statement of political philosophy, not itself an exemption from applicable laws. Read the transcript at the National Archives.

United States Constitution and Bill of Rights

Establish the federal constitutional structure and enumerate protections and limits on government authority. Constitution transcript · Bill of Rights transcript.

Murdock v. Pennsylvania, 319 U.S. 105 (1943)

The Supreme Court invalidated a flat licensing tax imposed as a condition of distributing religious literature. The holding concerns a particular burden on protected religious expression; it does not invalidate licensing requirements generally. Read the decision.

Shuttlesworth v. Birmingham, 394 U.S. 147 (1969)

The Court rejected an ordinance that gave officials unbridled discretion over permits for expressive marches. The decision recognizes that reasonable, objective regulations of streets and public safety may still apply. Read the decision.

Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1 (1968)

The Court addressed when an officer may briefly detain someone based on reasonable suspicion and conduct a limited protective frisk when there is reason to believe the individual is armed and dangerous. It recognizes both limits on government intrusion and circumstances permitting a stop and frisk. Read the decision.

These references provide context, not individualized legal advice. The effect of any law depends on its text, jurisdiction, and the circumstances. A personal statement of standing does not by itself remove otherwise applicable legal obligations.
